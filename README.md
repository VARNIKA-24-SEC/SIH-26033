## Problem Title
SIH 26033: Multiple intermediaries reduce farmers earnings and increase consumer prices.

## Problem Creater's Organization
Ministry of Consumer Affairs, Food & Public Distribution

## Theme
Agriculture, FoodTech & Rural Development

## Problem Description
Expected Solution: Create a digital marketplace that:

• Connects farmers/FPOs directly with consumers and bulk buyers.
• Provides logistics support.
• Uses AI for demand forecasting and route optimization.

Benefits:

• Better prices for farmers.
• Lower prices for consumers.
• Reduced supply chain inefficiencies.

## Proposed Solution
VYRA (Virtual Yield Reservation & Allocation) is an AI-powered agricultural marketplace designed to reduce unnecessary intermediaries by connecting farmers directly with consumers, retailers, restaurants, and bulk buyers.Unlike traditional marketplaces, VYRA introduces a Demand-First Harvest Reservation System, allowing buyers to reserve agricultural produce before it is harvested.

1. Digital Harvest Twin:
    Combines produce from multiple small farmers to fulfill large orders.Matches farmers and buyers based on quantity, location, price, and availability. Optimizes allocation to improve farmers' estimated net earnings.
2. Smart Harvest Reallocation:
    Automatically detects potential supply shortages.Suggests alternative farmers when expected harvest quantities change.Helps prevent order cancellations and supply disruptions.
3. Farmer Profit Shield:
    Compares buyer offers after accounting for transportation and handling costs.Helps farmers select offers that provide better net earnings.Provides transparent price information to support fair negotiations.
4. Second-Chance Harvest Exchange:
   Connects farmers with alternative buyers for unsold agricultural produce.Identifies suitable food-processing businesses and other buyers.Helps reduce agricultural waste and financial losses.

## Technical Approach
VYRA will be developed as a web and mobile-based platform with AI-powered forecasting, matching, and logistics optimization.

Step 1: Farmer Registration
Farmers register their location, crop details, expected yield, and harvesting period.

Step 2: Digital Harvest Prediction
The AI model estimates upcoming crop availability using historical production data, weather information, and farmer inputs.

Step 3: Buyer Demand Collection
Consumers and bulk buyers enter their requirements, preferred delivery dates, and quantities.

Step 4: AI Farmer-Buyer Allocation
The Harvest Puzzle Engine identifies suitable farmers and combines their expected produce to fulfill buyer requirements.

Step 5: Harvest Reservation
Buyers place advance reservations, and farmers confirm the quantities they can supply.

Step 6: Smart Logistics Optimization
The platform identifies suitable transportation options and calculates efficient delivery routes.

Step 7: Delivery and Payment
Produce is collected, quality and quantity are verified, and payments are processed through authorized payment services.

Step 8: Automatic Reallocation
If a farmer cannot fulfill a confirmed order, the system suggests alternative suppliers and updates the delivery plan after approval.

## Feasibility and Viability
Technical Feasibility:
  The platform can be developed using existing web technologies and open-source AI tools.Machine learning models can estimate agricultural demand and expected crop availability.Optimization algorithms can coordinate farmer allocations and transportation routes.A working prototype can be developed using simulated farmer, buyer, and logistics data.

Economic Feasibility:
  No large initial investment in physical infrastructure is required.Existing transportation networks can be used for agricultural deliveries.Cloud-based services can reduce initial development and maintenance costs.Revenue can be generated through transparent transaction fees and optional logistics services.

Operational Feasibility:
  Farmers can access the platform through a simple multilingual application.FPOs can assist farmers who have limited digital literacy.Buyers can reserve produce and track order status through a centralized dashboard.The platform can initially operate within a small agricultural region before expanding.

## Impact and Benefits
Economic Impact:
Higher farmer earnings: Reduces unnecessary intermediary charges and improves access to buyers.Lower consumer prices: Creates opportunities for direct purchasing and reduced supply-chain costs.Better price transparency: Farmers can compare offers based on estimated net earnings.Improved market access: Small farmers can collectively fulfill large orders.

Social Impact:
Empowers small and marginal farmers through digital market access.Encourages cooperation among farmers without requiring formal consolidation.Supports rural digital literacy and technology adoption.Creates opportunities for local logistics providers and agricultural entrepreneurs.

Environmental Impact:
Reduces agricultural waste through advance demand planning.Redirects suitable surplus produce to alternative buyers.Encourages efficient transportation through optimized delivery routes.Helps reduce unnecessary storage and transportation activities.
